Aemon Utility

Find the problem before it costs you the mix.

Put it first on any channel, or last on the master. It tells you in one line whether the track is mono-safe, balanced, clean and on target, and fixes what isn’t.

A mix check that talks

Mono safety, headroom, balance and loudness, in plain words and live. When something is off, the button to fix it is right there.

A stereo field you can grab

Drag the arc to pan and widen while you watch the phase correlation. Bass mono keeps the low end in the centre.

Loudness you can trust

Momentary, short-term, integrated and true peak to BS.1770, with streaming, club and broadcast targets.

Aemon Tune

In tune. Still yours.

The ring is your twelve notes, and one line goes from invisible to hard. It finds the key, says how sure it is, and leaves S sounds and breaths alone.

One line, not ten knobs

It starts as a free wavy voice and ends as steps. What the line looks like at your finger is what happens to the voice.

It tells you what it hears

The note, how far off it was, the key and how sure it is. Two voices at once, or the wrong key? It says so instead of guessing.

A note under the glass

In Pro you pull on the shape of one held note: the slide in, the landing, the vibrato. The curve shows it before you hear it.

Aemon EQ

The top end goes where you put it.

Most digital EQs squeeze their curves near 20 kHz. This one matches the analogue shape all the way up, with zero latency, and goes linear-phase when you need it.

Bands that only act when needed

Make any band dynamic and it moves only when its own range gets too loud. Tame a harsh note without dulling the rest.

Work in the sides, or one side

Put each band on stereo, left, right, mid or side. Clean the bass out of the sides, or open the top without touching the voice.

Helpers that listen

Tame harsh spots and clean up mud in one click, then hear the result at the same loudness with auto gain.

Aemon De-esser

Softer S. Same voice.

It tells an S from a vowel, and judges it against how loud the singer is at that moment. A quiet verse and a loud chorus get the same care, and vowels come out untouched.

Find my S

Let the voice play and press one button. It listens back over the last seconds, finds where this singer’s S sits and sets a safe amount.

Presets with a goal

“About 4 dB off the sharp ones” means about 4 dB. Each voice preset aims itself at your recording, from rap to podcasts to backing vocals.

Cut above, the S, or all of it

Dip everything above the S, only the S itself, or the whole voice. On stereo, the middle or the sides, with the removed sound on its own.

Aemon Compressor

Hear the punch. Not the level trick.

Clean compression with a display you can grab. Auto level keeps the output as loud as the input, so you judge the compression and nothing else.

One amount, five styles

Gentle, Punchy, Glue, Vocal and Smash. Turn one knob and read whether it’s subtle, healthy or too much.

Room to catch the peak

Up to 10 ms of lookahead, peak or RMS detection, auto release, and a side-chain high-pass so the kick stops driving everything.

External side-chain and mix

Duck the bass under the kick or the music under a voice. Blend in the dry signal for parallel compression in place.

Aemon Multiband

Every part of the sound, held on its own.

Two to five bands that turn loud parts down and bring quiet parts up. Choose a goal and it sets every line to the music you’re playing.

Pick a goal

Tighter lows, tame harsh, even and dense, or glue. Then Amount and Mix, and you’re done.

Fit to my music

Presets say how many dB they take off the loud parts. The plugin runs its own bands over the last eight seconds and finds the lines that do it.

Natural or straight phase

Zero-latency crossovers, or straight phase for mastering. Sidechain input, solo per band, and a mix that never combs.

Aemon Low End

Kick and 808, on the same side.

It measures what the two do to each other, in decibels, and tells you if they cancel. Then it lines them up and ducks only what clashes.

A reading you can trust

What it says equals the measured sum within 0.02 dB.

It knows when to leave it

A move counts only when it’s proven on hits it hasn’t seen. Otherwise it changes nothing and says why.

Duck only the clash

Only under the line, only while the kick is there, never deeper than you set.

Aemon Clipper

The peaks go. The punch stays.

It cuts the tallest instants so everything else can come up. Put it before the limiter and the limiter works less. From hard to soft, with a ceiling that holds between the samples.

Shave the peaks for me

Finds how much you can take off before you’d hear it: 3 dB at most, and no more than one percent of the sound.

Hard, round or soft

One Softness control. Hard stays untouched right up to the ceiling. Soft bends early and adds warmth.

Clean where it counts

8× oversampling and a second anti-aliasing method keep false tones low, 90 dB down on a hard-clipped 5 kHz tone. Hear removed plays exactly what was cut.

Aemon Limiter

Loud enough. Never too far.

Pick a target, press one button, and it pushes until the sound would start to suffer. Then it stops. The ceiling holds between the samples too.

Make it loud, safely

Streaming, club, broadcast or as loud as it stays clean. It measures your track, sets the push, and won’t crush it to reach a number.

It checks its own output

A second stage measures the limited signal with the official meter filter and trims the last tenths, so converters and codecs don’t clip.

Compare at equal loudness

One button plays the limited sound as quietly as the original, so bypass compares sound, never volume. Exports ignore it.

    Questions, answered.

    What’s the catch?

    There isn’t one. No strings, no login, no email, no data asked, no trial that runs out. I made them for the love of the game. The full plugins are free, for personal and paid work, with no time limit and no watermark. What you make with them belongs to you.

    Will it work in my DAW?

    If your DAW loads 64-bit VST3 plugins on Windows, yes. That covers most of them.

    Is there a Mac version?

    Not yet. The plugins are written to run on macOS too, and a Mac version (VST3 and AU) is planned after the Windows release.

    Why does Windows warn me when I install?

    Because the installer isn’t signed yet, and because it’s new. Signing is coming soon. This is what you’ll see and what to press. Only download Aemon from this page or from the project’s GitHub releases.

    Do the plugins collect anything?

    No. They never connect to the internet. This page has no cookies and no tracking either.

    Can I read or change the code?

    Yes. Aemon is open source under the AGPL-3.0 licence. You can study it, build it yourself, and share your changes under the same licence.

    Can I sing through them while I record?

    Most of them have no delay, or a few milliseconds. The ones that listen ahead say so: the bar at the bottom shows “live” or “mixing” with the delay, and a card tells you the first time. Tune has a Recording mode for this, with 12 to 21 ms depending on how low the voice goes.

    What comes next?

    All of them in one place. After that, something much bigger. Each one is tested like these, and comes out when it’s ready.
