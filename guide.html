Install

  1. Close your DAW and run Aemon-Suite-Setup.exe.
  2. If Windows says it protected your PC, choose More info, then Run anyway.
  3. When Windows asks to allow changes to your device, choose Yes.
  4. Tick the plugins you want and press Install.
  5. Open your DAW, scan for new plugins, and look for Aemon in the effects list.

Why the warnings? Aemon isn’t signed yet, so Windows can’t show who made it, and it warns about any new app that few people have installed. The second question is there because plugins go in a folder shared by every DAW on the computer. Signing is coming soon. See pictures of both

The plugins go to C:\Program Files\Common Files\VST3, where every VST3 host looks. To update, run the newer installer: it finds what you have, marks it and adds what’s new. To remove them, use Windows Settings, Apps, Aemon Suite. Your own presets are never touched.

No administrator rights? Run Aemon-Suite-Setup.exe /CURRENTUSER. It installs to the per-user VST3 folder, which many DAWs also scan.

By hand: unpack the ZIP and copy the .vst3 folders to the folder above.

The basics, in all fifteen

Simple and Pro

Switch at the top of the window. Both show the same settings, so switching never changes the sound. Simple has a few big controls, live checks in plain words and one-click fixes. Pro has everything.

Knobs and numbers

Drag up and down. Hold Shift for fine moves. Double-click to reset. Scroll to nudge. Click a number to type it.

Presets

Every preset is named after what it does. Use the arrows to step through, or click the name for the list. Save keeps your own, as files in Documents\Aemon. The word edited means you’ve changed something since loading.

Compare and undo

A and B hold two full settings, and A›B copies one across. Undo and redo are the arrows, or Ctrl Z and Ctrl Shift Z.

Live or mixing

The bar at the bottom shows the delay. live means you can play or sing through it. mixing means it listens ahead and delays the sound by more than 12 ms: your DAW lines it up when you mix, but while you record you’d hear yourself late. The first time, a card tells you.

Size and bypass

Click the percentage or drag the corner, from 60 to 200 %. The round button bypasses without a click.

Utility

For level, stereo, phase and loudness. It never colours the sound: at its defaults, what comes out is bit for bit what went in.

Simple

Mix check watches the output and tells you whether it’s mono-safe, has headroom, is balanced and sits on your loudness target. When something is off, its button fixes it. Match target loudness sets Volume and stops before peaks pass −1 dBFS. Check in mono lets you hear what a phone or a mono club system plays. Fix phase problem lights up when left and right cancel each other.

Pro

Gain, Pan, Width
±36 dB, four pan laws (0, −3, −4.5, −6 dB), and 0 to 200 % width.
Mono, Swap, Ø
Sum to mono, swap sides, or flip the polarity of either side.
Mid / Side
Listen to what both sides share, or only the difference. Encode and decode M/S for other plugins in between.
Bass mono
Everything below the chosen frequency becomes mono. The centre isn’t touched.
Loudness
Momentary, short-term, integrated and true peak to BS.1770, measured on the input or the output.

Tune

For one voice at a time. Teal is you, amber is what comes out. It works live: it changes what plays, it doesn’t edit the past.

Simple

The ring is the twelve notes. Lit notes are allowed, tap one to switch it. The line goes from Invisible over Natural, Polished and Tight to Hard. The key finds itself while the voice plays and says how sure it is. Not sure yet? Let it hear a little more, or choose the key yourself. I am recording has the least delay, mixing has the surest ear. Tell it how low the voice goes: the lower, the longer it has to listen. Throat makes the voice smaller or deeper without changing the notes.

Pro

The take
The last seconds, as sung and as tuned. Click a lane to switch a note, drag it for a few cents up or down, double-click it for its own strength.
A note under the glass
The last held note, large. Drag the points: how much of the slide in is kept, how fast it lands, when it lets go, how much vibrato stays, how hard it pulls.
Throat
Smaller or deeper, shown as the curve of the voice.
Notes come from
The notes that are on, or a MIDI note you hold.
Transpose, Mix, A4
Half tones, how much of the tuned voice, and the tuning of the song.
What it can’t do
Two voices at once: it says so. Two voices an octave apart, or a fourth or fifth equally loud, it can’t tell. An almost whispered voice is partly left alone rather than guessed. Delay for high, middle and low voices: 12, 14 or 21 ms in recording, 42, 47 or 60 ms in mixing.

EQ

Sixteen bands on a live spectrum. Double-click the display to add a band, drag it to set frequency and gain, scroll over it to change its width.

Simple

Low cut, Bass, Mud, Presence and Air, with a tone balance that compares your sound with a typical mix, vocal or bass. Tame harsh spots finds narrow peaks and places gentle dynamic cuts. Clean up mud finds where the low mids pile up.

Pro

Shapes
Bell, shelves, cuts, notch, band pass and tilt. Cuts go from 12 to 96 dB per octave.
Dynamic
The band only acts when its own range gets louder than the threshold. Good for harsh words or a boomy note.
Placement
Stereo, left, right, mid or side, per band.
Phase
Zero has no latency and is the default. Linear keeps timing between frequencies, and adds about 91 ms (medium) or 176 ms (high). Use it in mastering, rather than while you play.
Auto gain
Keeps the loudness the same so you judge the tone.

De-esser

For sharp S, T and SH sounds. It compares each S with how loud the voice is, so a quiet verse and a loud chorus are treated the same.

Simple

Play the voice, then press Find my S. It listens back over the last seconds and sets where to work and how much. Amount is how much of the excess is taken away, At most is the deepest cut allowed, so an S never becomes a lisp. Hear only the S plays what it has found. Less takes a fifth off. Voice presets aim themselves at your recording if it has played for a moment first.

Pro

Where
The frequency the S starts at, 3 to 12 kHz.
Line
How loud an S may be, against the voice or as a fixed level. Choose which with Voice and Fixed.
Strictness
How sure it must be that it’s an S. Above about 70 % bright vowels can count as one.
Cut
Above dips everything above the S, Just S only its own range, Whole the full voice.
Works on
Stereo, the middle or the sides.
Lookahead, Release
How early it starts and how fast it lets go.
Removed
Plays only what is taken away.

Compressor

The left display shows the level and how much it’s being turned down. On the right is the curve: drag the lower handle for the threshold and the upper one for the ratio.

Simple

Choose a Style, then turn Amount until it sounds controlled. Set amount for me measures what’s playing and aims for about 3 dB on the loud parts. Less pumping slows the release. The check tells you if it’s gentle, moderate or heavy. If you’ve changed something by hand, no style is highlighted.

Pro

Attack and release
A slow attack lets the hit through. A fast one holds it down. Auto release adapts to the music.
Lookahead
Up to 10 ms, to catch a peak before it arrives. Adds the same latency.
Side-chain high-pass
Stops low end from driving the compression.
Ext SC
Compress from another track. Route that track to the plugin’s side-chain input in your DAW.
Mix
Blends in the uncompressed signal, for parallel compression.
Auto level
Keeps the output as loud as the input.

Rider

It rides a vocal like a hand on the fader. Breaths, S sounds and silence don’t move it.

Simple

Press Set it for me while the voice plays. The orange words are the settings: how even, how far, how fast, and how much to follow the beat (send the beat to its sidechain). Gentler takes some off.

Pro

Ride, Write, Play
Ride works live. Write sends the moves to your DAW as automation of the Ride parameter. Play follows what’s written. A project never opens in Write.
Look ahead
150 ms, so a word is caught before it starts. Singing through it? Use the preset for recording: no delay.
Listening
Voice only, or anything. What is further under the line than you say is left alone.
Good to know
It doesn’t limit peaks. Put a limiter after it if peaks matter.

Multiband

A compressor for each part of the sound. Two to five bands, each turning loud parts down and, if you want, quiet parts up.

Simple

Choose a goal: Tighter lows, Tame harsh, Even & dense or Glue. Play the loudest part for a few seconds and press Fit to my music: it sets every line so the goal does what it says. Amount scales everything, Mix blends in the uncompressed sound, Less takes a quarter off.

Pro

Bands
Two to five. Drag the dividers on the display. 24 or 48 dB per octave.
Down and up
Threshold and ratio for loud parts, and a second pair that raises quiet parts. The up line always stays below the down line, so a band that is compressed most of the time comes up less than the preset asks for.
Phase
Natural has no latency. Straight keeps timing between bands and adds latency: use it for mastering.
Solo, On
Hear one band, or leave a band as it is.
Sidechain
Another track drives the bands. Route it to the plugin’s sidechain input in your DAW.
Auto level
Keeps the output as loud as the input.

Transient

More or less hit, more or less of what follows. No threshold: quiet and loud hits get the same shape.

Simple

Attack is the hit, Body is what comes after. Focus works on lows, mids or highs only. Compare at equal loudness keeps it fair.

Pro

Lengths
How long the hit and the body are.
Same peaks
Sets the output so peaks stay where they were.
Hear change
Plays only what it changes.

Saturation

Adds harmonics: warmth, weight, presence or dirt. The level is matched, so you judge the colour.

Simple

Pick a character, Warm, Thick, Bright or Broken, and what it works on: everything, lows, mids or highs. Set amount for me finds an amount you can notice without it getting harsh. Cleaner backs off 3 dB. Choosing a focus is the quickest way to get an 808 heard on phones without touching the rest.

Pro

Drive
How hard the sound is pushed into the curve, up to 30 dB.
Bias
More even harmonics or more odd ones.
Tone
A darker or brighter result.
Mix
Blends in the clean sound, in time, so nothing combs.
Oversampling
8× is the default. Lower is lighter on the CPU and less clean.
Level matched
Keeps the result as loud as the original.

Low End

Put it on the 808 or the bass. Send the kick to its sidechain.

Simple

It shows both, and what they do together. Line them up moves the bass only when that’s proven to help, and says why when it isn’t. Duck what clashes makes room only where both are. Put it back undoes the move.

Pro

Delay, Turn, Flip
The move itself, by hand.
Deepest, Below, Release
How far it ducks, under which frequency, and how fast it comes back.
Good to know
Delay 58 ms, so keep it for mixing. It uses about 2 % of a core at 48 kHz and 8 % at 192 kHz.

Imager

Makes things wider or narrower. It only touches the difference between left and right, so the mono mix stays the same.

Simple

Width from 0 to 200 %. Low end brings everything below a frequency to the middle. Make stereo opens up a mono sound. Keep it mono-safe holds what a band may lose in mono. Hear it in mono and Only the sides are for listening.

Pro

Bands
Four, with a width each. Drag the dividers.
Mono-safe limit
How many dB a band may lose on a mono speaker.
Centre
Pulls a lopsided sound back to the middle. Centre it measures and sets it.
Quality
Straight adds 48 ms and has the cleanest bands. Zero delay is for playing live.
Equal loudness
For comparing while you listen. Exports ignore it.

Reverb

Six spaces. The dry sound is never delayed.

Simple

Choose a space, then Decay, Duck and Mix. Keep the words clear sets the gap, the low cut and the ducking. On a send plays only the space. Freeze holds it.

Pro

The picture
Drag the gap, the decay, the top and the bottom.
What goes in
Low cut and high cut before the space.
Words
How far it steps back while the dry sound plays, and how fast it returns.
Gate, Grit
The 80s snare, and dirt on the tail.

Delay

Echoes in time with your song, or in milliseconds.

Simple

Time, Feedback, Duck and Mix. Hold Throw on a word, or automate it, and that word echoes.

Pro

Character
Digital is clean, Tape is softer and moves, Lo-fi is crushed.
Glide or Fade
What happens when you change the time while it plays: a pitch slide, or a fade.
Throws only
Nothing echoes but what’s thrown.
Feedback
Up to 110 %. The echoes never pass −3 dB.

Clipper

Cuts the tallest peaks. Use it before the limiter, or on drums.

Simple

Push drives the peaks over the ceiling, Softness sets how gently they bend, Ceiling is the highest level. Shave the peaks for me finds how much you can take off before you’d hear it. Less backs off 2 dB. Compare at equal loudness plays the result as loud as the original.

Pro

Shape
Hard, Round and Soft set the softness.
Stereo link
At 100 % both sides are cut together, so the image stays put.
Mix
Blends in the sound before clipping. Below 100 % peaks can pass the ceiling.
Oversampling
8× is the default and the cleanest.
Hear removed
Plays exactly what is cut.

Limiter

Goes last on the master. Pick where the music is going, and it handles the level.

Simple

Choose Streaming (−14 LUFS), Club (−9), Broadcast (−23) or Loudest clean. Make it loud safely measures your track and sets Push, and stops short if reaching the target would hurt the sound. Compare at equal loudness turns the limited sound down to the level of the original. Then bypass compares sound, never volume. It only works while you listen: exports ignore it.

Play the loudest part for a few seconds before pressing Make it loud safely. It measures what it just heard. Choosing a loudness preset while music plays does the same.

Pro

Push
How hard you drive into the limiter.
Ceiling
The highest the output can go. −1 dBTP is the safe choice for streaming.
Style
Sets the release: Clean for mastering, Punchy for drums, Dense for loud modern masters, Safe for gentle protection. Turn Release yourself and no style is highlighted.
Lookahead
Sees peaks coming. Changing it changes the latency, so the sound fades for a moment.
True peak
Catches peaks between the samples, at 2×, 4× or 8×. 4× is a good default.
Hear removed
Plays only what the limiter takes away.
Dither
16-bit or 24-bit, only on your final export.

A good order

On the master: Utility to check mono and level, EQ to fix the balance, Compressor or Multiband for a little glue, a touch of Saturation, Imager for width, Clipper to shave the peaks, Limiter last. On a voice: Utility, Tune early, EQ, Rider, Compressor, De-esser, Saturation, with Reverb and Delay on sends. On drums: Transient before the Compressor. On the 808: Low End, with the kick on its sidechain. You’ll rarely need all of them. Trust your ears over the order. New to mixing? Read Mixing, start to finish.

If something’s wrong

The plugins don’t show up
Scan again in your DAW, and make sure it’s a 64-bit host that loads VST3.
Setup says a plugin is open
Save your project, close your DAW and press Retry. Setup never closes programs for you.
The sound is delayed
Some plugins listen ahead: the bar at the bottom says “mixing” and how much. Your DAW lines it up during playback. Use zero-latency modes and the Recording settings of Tune and Rider while recording.
